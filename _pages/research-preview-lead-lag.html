Research question

Price discovery is not necessarily single-scale

A venue can react first at sub-second horizons yet differ at slower adjustment scales. Standard synchronized-return correlations can also distort inference when trade arrivals are irregular.

Why asynchronous estimation?

Binance and Kraken do not print trades on a common clock. Previous-tick interpolation creates a synthetic synchronous series and can shift or flatten the estimated lag. The Hayashi–Yoshida covariance instead works directly with overlapping observation intervals.

Why multiple scales?

Daubechies wavelet filters split the lagged covariance contrast into dyadic frequency bands. The result is a lead–lag profile, not one aggregate number: faster bands describe immediate price discovery while coarser bands capture slower cross-venue adjustment.

Estimator pipeline

From raw event time to a scale-by-scale lag

Ingest event-time data

Normalize public exchange trades without forcing a shared timestamp grid.

Build lagged HY contrast

Shift the second series over candidate lags and accumulate overlapping return intervals.

Apply wavelet filters

Use Daubechies coefficients and autocorrelation wavelets to isolate dyadic scales.

Estimate each maximum

Select the lag maximizing the absolute filtered contrast at every scale.

Sign convention

Series 1 is written first. A negative estimate means series 1 leads series 2; a positive estimate means series 2 leads series 1. This convention was unit-tested and audited with shifted synthetic data before interpreting exchange results.

Core empirical result

Binance leads Kraken across all tested BTC scales

The ridge of contrast maxima remains on the negative-lag side. Its magnitude grows at coarser scales, from −0.05s at the fastest band to −1.95s at the slowest band.

Robustness and breadth

Validation protocol

Designed to falsify the result

  • Deterministic sign-convention tests with known shifts.
  • Monte Carlo diagnostics under constant and Heston-type volatility.
  • Lo–MacKinlay asynchronous sampling versus previous-tick interpolation.
  • Tick-density artifact tests to separate activity differences from genuine lead–lag structure.
  • LOBSTER midpoint sanity check on equity order-book data.

Limits

Statistical dependence is not free arbitrage

The public-data implementation uses trade log-prices and a 50ms lag grid, not the quote microprice and ultra-fine timestamps of the original empirical setting. A measured lead does not include fees, latency, slippage, queue priority, inventory or capital constraints. Deployability requires a separate execution study.

Data universe

Cross-venue, cross-product and sanity-check samples

Binance spot, USDT-M and COIN-M aggregate trades; Kraken spot trades; Bybit public trade archives; and LOBSTER message/order-book data for a quote-based equity check. The core April 2026 Binance–Kraken sample contains approximately 12.6 million Binance BTC observations and 0.86 million Kraken observations.

Reproducibility

Research artifacts preserved

The project includes configurable experiments, cached summaries, figures, tests and a complete 31-page paper. This case study uses the original generated figures without redrawing the results.
